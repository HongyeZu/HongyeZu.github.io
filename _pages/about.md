I am a recent MSc graduate in Interactive Media from University College Cork, with an interdisciplinary academic background spanning computer science, communication, and interactive media.

My academic interests centre on the relationship between digital technologies, people, society, and the environment. My research experience spans both qualitative communication research and the design and evaluation of interactive systems, allowing me to approach research questions from both social and technical perspectives.

During my MSc in Communication, I investigated self-presentation on social media, examining how young Chinese TikTok vloggers construct and manage their online identities, their motivations for self-presentation, and its impact on their everyday lives. This research involved in-depth interviews, thematic analysis, and the application of Goffman's Dramaturgical Theory.

More recently, during my MSc in Interactive Media, I explored technology-mediated environmental participation through the design and development of a smartphone-based crowdsensing system for light pollution monitoring. The project combined mobile application development, environmental data collection, geospatial visualization, and usability evaluation.

Across these experiences, I have developed a broader interest in human-computer interaction, digital and social media, data visualization, social computing, and user-centred technologies for addressing environmental and social questions.

## Research Projects

### Urban Light Pollution Visualization via Crowdsourced Smartphone Imaging

As part of my MSc in Interactive Media at University College Cork, I designed and developed a smartphone-based crowdsensing system for participatory light pollution monitoring.

The system included a native iOS application developed in Swift for capturing night-time images with automatic GPS tagging, timestamping, and on-device brightness estimation. I also developed a Node.js and MongoDB backend and a Mapbox GL JS web interface to visualize crowdsourced observations as spatial heatmaps.

I piloted the system in Harbin, China, and conducted a usability study to evaluate its ease of use and the accessibility of the visualization. The project explored the feasibility, spatiotemporal analytical value, and participatory challenges of using smartphones for environmental monitoring.


### Vloggers' Self-Presentation on TikTok: Motives and Impact

As part of my MSc in Communication at Taylor's University, I conducted a qualitative study of self-presentation among young Chinese TikTok vloggers.

Drawing on Goffman's Dramaturgical Theory, the study examined how vloggers construct self-presentation, what motivates these practices, and how sustained self-presentation affects their everyday lives.

I conducted in-depth interviews and applied thematic analysis to examine participants' experiences. The study identified themes related to stage-shaping behaviours, social connection, monetization, self-realization, and the positive and negative impacts of sustained online self-presentation.
