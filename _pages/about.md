I am a recent MSc graduate in Interactive Media from University College Cork, with an interdisciplinary academic background in computer science, communication, and interactive media.

My academic interests centre on how digital technologies interact with people, society, and the environment. My research experience spans qualitative communication research and the design and evaluation of interactive systems, allowing me to approach research questions from both social and technical perspectives.

My previous research has explored self-presentation and identity on social media, as well as smartphone-based crowdsensing and data visualization for environmental monitoring. Through these experiences, I have developed a broader interest in human-computer interaction, digital and social media, data visualization, and user-centred technologies.

## Selected Research Projects

### Urban Light Pollution Visualization via Crowdsourced Smartphone Imaging

*University College Cork | MSc Interactive Media*

I designed and developed a smartphone-based crowdsensing system for participatory light pollution monitoring. The project combined an iOS application for collecting geotagged night-time images with a backend system and a web-based heatmap visualization.

The research explored the feasibility and participatory potential of using everyday smartphones for environmental monitoring, alongside the usability of the system and its visualization.


### Vloggers' Self-Presentation on TikTok: Motives and Impact

*Taylor's University | MSc Communication*

I conducted a qualitative study examining how young Chinese TikTok vloggers construct and manage their online self-presentation.

Drawing on Goffman's Dramaturgical Theory, the research used in-depth interviews and thematic analysis to explore self-presentation strategies, motivations, and the perceived impacts of sustained online self-presentation.
